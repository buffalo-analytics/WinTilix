# Researching a domain before designing its team

Do this for every domain without a preset. The aim is a team that mirrors how a competent organisation in that field really works, with the gates and standards that organisation answers to. Budget a handful of searches, not an essay.

## 1. Pin down the work

- What is being produced (a design package, a review report, a certified dossier, a deployed system)?
- Who consumes it and what decision does it support? Who signs it off, and is that person the user, a qualified professional, or a regulator?
- Which region and jurisdiction? Standards and law differ (for this user, check South African and SANS adoption of ISO/IEC standards as well as EU or US ones where relevant).

## 2. Find the real lifecycle and gates

Search for the field's standard process: V-model, stage-gate, systematic review protocol, clinical evaluation, audit cycle. List the gates (requirements frozen, design review, verification, release). Each gate becomes either a coordinator rule or a reviewer's job.

## 3. Find the governing standards and guidance

- List the ISO/IEC/national standards, legislation and professional frameworks that apply, each with the edition you found.
- Prefer primary sources (the standards body, the regulator, the professional college) over blog summaries. If only a secondary source is reachable, mark the item unverified in the spec.
- Decide which of these a standards or compliance seat should own, and which belong to the discipline specialists.

## 4. Find the typical roles and what each is accountable for

Look at how teams, job descriptions or review boards in this field divide the work. Note the separation of duties the field insists on (designer is not approver, author is not reviewer). Those separations become the read-only seats.

## 5. Find the failure modes

Search for common causes of rework, recalls, retractions or incidents in the field. Each recurring failure suggests a task or a limit in some seat's prompt (for example unit errors, unvalidated models, unsupported causal claims, untraceable requirements).

## 6. Check the tools and artefacts

What do real practitioners produce and use: calculation sheets, FMEA, traceability matrix, test records, PRISMA flow, search logs? Name them in `tasks` so agents produce them.

## 7. Record what you based it on

Put a short note of sources and any unverified items in the spec's `summary`, or tell the user in the roster summary. A team built on unstated research cannot be trusted or updated.

## Then staff it

Start from the standard roster in `role-library.md`, replace the makers with the field's disciplines, add the standards or ethics seat if regulated, and add the specialist critic that matches the field's worst failure mode. Cut to a core team and mark the rest optional.
